  • Sep 2019 – Dec 2020, Jun 2020-Aug 2020, Jun 2021-Aug 2021, Jun 2022-Aug 2022, Jun 2023-Aug 2023, Jan 2024 – Current
  • Using Remote Sensing to Reveal Factors Determining Wild Blueberry Productivity.: Using satellite-based remote sensing data, this project aims to assess the historical pattern of photosynthetic activity-related variables and their relationships with climatic variables to understand the resilience of wild blueberries to warming.
  • Remote Sensing of Spatial Variation of Leaf Nutrient Status and Leaf Water Status of Wild Blueberries for Precision Management.: Using high-resolution airborne hyperspectral imagery, this project aims to develop a remotely sensed, non-destructive, and cost-effective method for predicting leaf nutrient status and water status and their spatial variation within a large commercial field.
  • Development of Non-destructive Turgor Loss Point Prediction Model Using Leaf Spectral Responses and Machine Learning.: This project aims to use contact hyperspectral sensing to detect leaf spectral properties associated with the physiological properties of the turgor loss point.
  • A Satellite-based Remote Sensing Platform to Understand Crop Physiological Anomaly Caused Due to Recent Summer Drought on Wild Blueberry in Maine.: Used MODIS satellite-based multi-year and multi-spectral imagery platforms to understand crop health and vegetation anomaly caused by drought and its impact on production.
  • Detection of Spatial Variation in Wild Blueberries Water Stress using UAV-borne Thermal Sensor.: Used UAV-based high-resolution thermal imagery to monitor water stress for precision irrigation in wild blueberries.
